# Overnight Pepper Kugel

Jerusalem kugel with spaghetti squash for noodles and boiled-down orange for
caramel, left in a low oven overnight. Serves 8 as a side. Start the evening
before.

## Ingredients

- 1 spaghetti squash, about 4 lb (1.8 kg)
- 4 eggs
- 2 cups (475 ml) orange juice, from about 6 oranges
- 5 tbsp (75 ml) olive oil
- 2 tsp coarse black pepper
- 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt

## Steps

1. Oven to 375 F. Halve the squash crosswise, which keeps the strands long. Seed
   it and roast cut side down 40-50 min, until the strands pull loose.
2. Fork out the strands and squeeze them in a towel, a handful at a time, until
   nothing runs. You want about 4 cups. Wet strands make custard, not kugel.
3. Boil the orange juice down to 1/4 cup, dark and thick as honey. Watch the
   last few minutes, it burns. Off heat, stir in 4 tbsp of the oil.
4. Toss the strands with the syrup, pepper and salt. Let it cool until just
   warm, about 10 min, so the eggs do not scramble. Beat the eggs and mix in.
5. Oil a heavy 7-8 in lidded pot with the rest of the oil and line it with
   parchment, bottom and sides. Pack the kugel in, a circle of parchment on top,
   lid on.
6. 325 F for 30 min, then 225 F overnight, 6 h at least.
7. Lid and top parchment off, 350 F, 30-45 min, until the top is dark and the
   loose liquid is gone.
8. Cool 15 min, invert onto a plate, cut in wedges. Hot or room temperature.

## Notes

- The real one takes up to 1 1/2 cups of sugar, most of it cooked to caramel.
  Orange boiled to syrup should bring the color and some of the bitter edge, and
  a fraction of the sweetness. Pepper leads.
- Sugar caramel seizes when it meets cool squash, which is the failure people
  report on the squash versions. Orange syrup does not set hard.
- The overnight oven is from noodle kugel. No squash version found goes
  overnight. They bake 45 min at 350-375 F, and 350 F in a 9x13 in pan is the
  fallback. Step 7 is here because squash is wetter than noodles.
