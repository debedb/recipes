# Tzimmes (цимес)

Carrots and dried fruit stewed in honey until syrupy. Sweet, served warm or at
room temperature with tea. Not the brisket braise - no meat here.

Serves 5-6 as a sweet.

## Ingredients

- 2 lb / 900 g carrots, peeled, cut into 1/4 in / 5 mm coins
- 1 cup pitted prunes (~170 g), halved
- 1 cup dried apricots (~140 g), halved
- 1/2 cup raisins (~75 g)
- 1.3 oz (37 g) honey, plus more to taste
- 3 tbsp butter, or neutral oil to keep it parve
- 1/2 tsp cinnamon
- 1/4 tsp salt
- 2 1/2 cups water
- Juice of 1/2 lemon

## Steps

1. Carrots, water, butter and salt in a wide pot. Cover, simmer 15-20 min,
   until the carrots give to a knife but are not soft.
2. Add prunes, apricots, raisins, honey, cinnamon. Simmer uncovered, stirring
   now and then, until the carrots are fully soft and the liquid has reduced
   to a syrup that coats them. Expect longer than the 25-30 min this started
   out calling for: there is a cup more water to drive off and less than half
   the honey to thicken what is left. Go by the spoon, not the clock.
3. Watch the last 10 min. Once the liquid is mostly gone the syrup scorches
   fast. Carrots still firm and pot going dry: add a splash of water.
4. Off heat, stir in lemon juice. Taste. More honey if the fruit was sour;
   more lemon if it is cloying.

## Notes

- Only the water and the honey were recorded from the cook. Every other
  quantity here is still the synthesized starting point, not a weighed
  amount, so treat them as a starting point still.
- The honey came down from the 1/4 cup this began with, about 3 oz at honey's
  density, to 1.3 oz - less than half - while the water went up a full cup.
  Both changes push the same way: more liquid to drive off, less sugar to
  thicken what remains. If it will not come to a syrup that coats, it needs
  more time on the heat, not more honey, unless you want it sweeter.

## Serving

Warm or room temperature, small bowls, with tea. Chopped walnuts on top if you
want the crunch. Keeps a week refrigerated and is better on the second day.

## Sources

Traditional Ashkenazi sweet tzimmes. No single source. The quantities began
as a starting point; the water and the honey have since been corrected
against an actual cook, the rest have not.
