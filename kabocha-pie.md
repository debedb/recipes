# Kabocha Pie

Sweet potato and coconut custard set inside a whole kabocha, which is the dish
and the crust. Serves 8 as dessert. Start the day before.

## Ingredients

- 1 kabocha, about 2 1/2 lb (1.1 kg)
- 1 sweet potato, about 8 oz (225 g)
- 3 eggs
- 2/3 cup (160 ml) full-fat coconut milk
- 1 cup (240 ml) orange juice, from about 3 oranges
- 1 tsp orange zest
- 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1/4 tsp ground ginger
- 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
- 1/8 tsp ground cloves
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt

## Steps

1. Oven to 275 F. Roast the sweet potato whole, unwrapped, on foil, 2 to 2 1/2
   h, until very soft. Peel and puree. You want 2/3 cup.
2. Boil the orange juice down to 2 tbsp. Watch the end, it burns. Cool.
3. Cut a circle around the kabocha's stem, wide enough for a spoon, and keep the
   cut-off top. If the rind fights the knife, pierce it and microwave 3 min
   first. Scoop out the seeds and strings and leave the walls alone.
4. Set up the steamer with the kabocha empty. In a deep pot with a lid, put
   about 1 in of water and a rack, or three balls of foil. Lay two long strips
   of folded foil over it, crossed, ends up the sides: a sling to lift by. On
   that, a heatproof plate or shallow bowl, clear of the water and narrower than
   the pot, so steam gets past. Stand the kabocha on it, the cut-off top beside
   it. Check that the pot lid closes and the sling lifts. If the lid will not
   close, a large metal bowl upside down can be the lid.
5. Whisk the puree, coconut milk, eggs, orange syrup, zest, spices and salt.
   Strain. Pour into the kabocha where it sits, to 1/2 in below the rim; it
   swells. Steam any extra in a cup.
6. Pot lid on. Bring the water to a boil, turn it down to a simmer and keep it
   there. High heat can split the custard. Top up with boiling water before it
   runs low.
7. Check from 45 min after it simmers; expect 1 to 1 1/2 h. The custard is done
   when its middle reads 170 F or a skewer in it comes out clean. Then push a
   skewer into the side: it should slide through the wall. Not before, or the
   custard runs out of the hole.
8. Off the heat. Tilt the pot lid away from you, let the steam clear, then lift
   the plate out by the sling, with mitts. Cool to room temperature, refrigerate
   overnight. Cut into 8 wedges. The skin is edible.

## Notes

- No sugar. One version of the Thai original, sangkaya fak thong, takes 180 g of
  palm sugar to 4 eggs and 200 ml of coconut milk. The slow-roasted sweet potato
  and the boiled-down orange stand in for it, and it will be a lot less sweet.
- Sources steam the whole squash 20 to 45 min and none of them weighed it. The 1
  to 1 1/2 h here is an estimate for this size with a thicker custard, so check
  from 45 min and go by the thermometer.
- 170 F, the high-heat warning and the top steamed beside the squash are Cooking
  with Lane's; Thai Food Online puts the top back on. The plate-and-foil steamer
  is Food Network's, the upside-down bowl The Takeout's. The wall test, the
  sling, topping up and filling in place are added here. The sources test only
  the custard.
